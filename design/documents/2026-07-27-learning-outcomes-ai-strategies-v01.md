# Aligning Department GenAI Commitments with Spanish Learning Outcomes

**Purpose:** Supporting guidance for drafting the Department of Spanish Statement on AI and Practices and Policies document.

**Status:** Working document. These strategies do not represent approved departmental policy.

## Guiding principle

Department learning outcomes should provide the pedagogical basis for decisions about GenAI. GenAI use should support, reveal, or assess student progress toward named outcomes rather than substitute for the student performance those outcomes require.

For each proposed commitment or rule, ask:

> What departmental learning outcome does this protect or advance, and what student performance must remain visible?

If a clear answer is unavailable, the commitment or rule may need revision.

## Opportunities for the public Statement on AI

The official document title retains “AI”; this section concerns GenAI.

### Organize commitments around departmental learning goals

The statement can connect GenAI use to three established goals:

- Communicative proficiency in Spanish
- Cultural analysis, contextualization, and reflection
- Analysis and contextualization of language variation

Using these goals would distinguish the statement from generic institutional GenAI guidance and explain how departmental commitments arise from the purposes of Spanish study.

### Distinguish educational support from substitution

The statement can acknowledge that GenAI may support practice, feedback, comparison, and inquiry while making clear that it cannot substitute for students' demonstrated ability to:

- Interpret complex spoken and written Spanish
- Sustain spontaneous conversations and ask probing questions
- Present organized ideas across multiple time frames
- Analyze cultural artifacts, contexts, voices, and perspectives
- Analyze linguistic forms and language variation
- Reflect on intercultural interaction, language development, and goals for improvement

Polished GenAI-generated language is not evidence that a student has achieved ACTFL Advanced Low proficiency or another departmental outcome.

### Protect spontaneous human interaction

SLO 1.2 provides a strong basis for preserving unscripted conversation, probing questions, audience adaptation, and real-time negotiation of meaning. GenAI conversation may offer supplemental practice, but it should not replace human interaction when interpersonal proficiency is the learning goal.

### Present AI literacy as critical comparison

GenAI output can serve as an object of disciplined inquiry. Students can evaluate its:

- Main claims, supporting evidence, organization, and chronology
- Register and suitability for an audience
- Descriptive or prescriptive assumptions about Spanish
- Representation of dialect, sociolect, and language variation
- Treatment of cultural voice, perspective, and context
- Representation or omission of historically marginalized communities

This approach connects AI literacy directly to interpretive communication, presentational communication, cultural analysis, and language analysis.

### Connect responsible GenAI use to reflection

SLOs 2.3 and 3.3 support a conception of AI literacy that includes humility, awareness of cultural assumptions, audience adaptation, self-evaluation, and goal setting. Students should consider not only whether GenAI output is accurate, but also how using a system affects their judgment, language development, and relationships with others.

## Opportunities for the Practices and Policies document

### Require outcome-linked assignment policies

Assignment guidance should identify:

1. Learning outcome being practiced or assessed
2. GenAI functions permitted
3. GenAI functions prohibited
4. Student performance that must remain independent and visible
5. Disclosure, verification, or process evidence required

This structure gives a pedagogical rationale for GenAI rules and avoids unsupported labels such as "AI allowed" or "AI prohibited."

### Identify non-delegable performance

Policies can distinguish whole-assignment restrictions from specific components students must perform themselves. A non-delegable component is one that directly demonstrates a departmental or course learning outcome.

Examples include:

- Interpreting a text before consulting a GenAI summary
- Responding spontaneously during an oral assessment
- Formulating and defending a thesis
- Selecting and explaining supporting evidence
- Analyzing a linguistic form and showing the reasoning used
- Reflecting on personal intercultural experience or language growth

This approach allows purposeful GenAI support without obscuring who achieved the assessed outcome.

### Develop safeguards by learning-outcome area

#### Interpretive communication

Students should personally identify main ideas, supporting details, chronology, and evidence when those abilities are assessed. GenAI summaries may be useful for later comparison, but should not replace initial interpretation.

#### Interpersonal communication

Assess spontaneous interaction live or under conditions that preserve real-time language production. Generated scripts should be prohibited when spontaneity and negotiation of meaning are assessed.

#### Presentational communication

Preserve evidence of student thesis formation, selection of evidence, organization, transitions, and spoken or written language production. Permitted assistance should reflect assignment goals and student proficiency level.

#### Cultural analysis and contextualization

Require artifact-based evidence, responsible contextual sourcing, and comparison of perspectives. Students remain responsible for detecting fabricated context, flattened cultural differences, and unsupported claims.

#### Language analysis and contextualization

Require students to justify phonological, morphological, syntactic, and sociolinguistic claims using appropriate terminology and evidence. GenAI-generated explanations should not replace analytical reasoning.

#### Cultural and language reflection

Keep self-assessment, personal intercultural reflection, and goal setting substantially student-authored. GenAI may help students organize ideas if permitted, but should not manufacture experiences, feelings, or claims of personal growth.

### Use rubric-aligned process evidence

Process evidence should demonstrate relevant learning rather than enable unnecessary surveillance. Suitable evidence may include:

- Annotations identifying thesis, chronology, or supporting details
- Oral follow-up explaining an interpretation or analytical choice
- Drafts showing development of thesis and organization
- Source notes connecting cultural claims to historical or social context
- Linguistic analysis showing intermediate reasoning
- Revision commentary identifying strengths, limits, and goals

Prompt logs and complete interaction histories should be required only when they serve a clear pedagogical purpose and comply with privacy expectations.

### Treat GenAI-generated Spanish as linguistic and cultural data

Guided activities can ask students to investigate whether GenAI systems:

- Over-standardize Spanish
- Erase dialect, sociolect, or register differences
- Favor prescriptive judgments over descriptive analysis
- Infer speaker identity too confidently from language features
- Misrepresent historically marginalized populations
- Flatten cultural voices, perspectives, or contexts

Such activities transform limitations of GenAI systems into direct practice for cultural and linguistic analysis.

### Preserve human and community interaction

For oral proficiency, community engagement, OPE courses, and intercultural reflection, GenAI interaction should not substitute for engagement with people when human interaction is integral to the outcome. Students and faculty should not upload identifiable recordings, transcripts, or community information to GenAI systems without appropriate authorization and consent.

### Coordinate policies where shared assessment requires comparability

Common GenAI expectations are especially important where courses contribute shared evidence of departmental outcomes. Priority areas include:

- SPA 315–319 regional courses
- SPA 320 Introduction to Linguistics
- SPA 330–389 seminars
- SPA OPE courses
- SPA 396 and Assessment Day

Coordination should protect validity and equity while preserving instructor discretion where assignments or learning goals differ.

### Provide outcome-aligned alternatives

Students who cannot or choose not to use a GenAI system should have an accessible alternative that addresses the same learning outcome. GenAI use should not become an unstated additional learning outcome unless the department explicitly adopts it as one.

## Drafting implications

The public Statement on AI should explain these commitments in affirmative, accessible language. It should emphasize human communication, cultural understanding, linguistic diversity, critical judgment, and responsibility.

The Practices and Policies document should translate those commitments into assignment design, disclosure expectations, assessment safeguards, privacy protections, and shared procedures. Detailed rules should remain proportional to the learning outcome and should preserve clear distinctions among departmental requirements, common-course agreements, and instructor discretion.

## Suggested next steps

- Map each section of both working outlines to relevant SLOs and rubric criteria.
- Identify assessment components that faculty consider non-delegable.
- Develop model assignment language using the outcome-linked policy structure.
- Review common assessments for consistency, validity, privacy, accessibility, and workload.
- Decide whether critical AI literacy will become an explicit departmental outcome or remain a means of advancing current outcomes.
