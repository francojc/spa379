# Spanish Department Learning Outcomes

## SLO 1: Language Proficiency

Upon completion of the Spanish major, students will be able to communicate effectively in Spanish in a variety of contexts according to ACTFL criteria for the Advanced Low level.

### 1.1 Interpretive communication

**Outcome:** Understand the main message and supporting details on a wide variety of topics across various time frames from complex, organized texts that are spoken and written.

**Assessment:**

- Oral/written comprehension of narrative texts: SPA 330–369, literature and culture seminars (criteria 1.1.1–1.1.3)
- Oral/written comprehension of informational texts: SPA 370–389, linguistics and language seminars (criteria 1.1.4–1.1.5)

**Rubric questions:**

In a complex, organized narrative, can the student:

- **1.1.1:** Identify the setting of the story: who, when, where, and why? (Y/N)
- **1.1.2:** Identify key moments or summarize main events in the plot? (Y/N)
- **1.1.3:** Establish the chronological sequence of events? (Y/N)

In a complex, organized informational text on a familiar or general-interest topic, can the student:

- **1.1.4:** Restate the main thesis? (Y/N)
- **1.1.5:** Identify subarguments or pieces of supporting evidence? (Y/N)

### 1.2 Interpersonal communication

**Outcome:** Maintain spontaneous conversations and discussions across various time frames on familiar and unfamiliar concrete topics using a series of connecting sentences and probing questions.

**Assessment:** ACTFL Oral Proficiency Interview (OPI), Assessment Day (SPA 396)

**Rubric question:**

- **1.2.1:** In an ACTFL Oral Proficiency Interview, can the student reach Advanced Low? (Y/N)

**Scoring levels:**

1. Novice High
2. Intermediate Low
3. Intermediate Mid
4. Intermediate High
5. Advanced Low
6. Advanced Mid
7. Advanced High
8. Superior
9. Distinguished

### 1.3 Presentational communication

**Outcome:** Produce detailed and organized presentations on familiar as well as unfamiliar concrete topics, in paragraphs and using various time frames, through spoken and written language.

**Assessment:** Oral/written argumentative presentation, SPA 330–389 seminars (criteria 1.3.4–1.3.6)

**Rubric questions:**

In a written or oral narrative about a personal experience, can the student:

- **1.3.1:** Describe the setting: who, when, where, and why? (Y/N)
- **1.3.2:** Recount a series of events? (Y/N)
- **1.3.3:** Establish the sequence or chronology of events? (Y/N)

In an informational or argumentative presentation or essay on a concrete topic, can the student:

- **1.3.4:** State a thesis or viewpoint? (Y/N)
- **1.3.5:** Provide supporting evidence? (Y/N)
- **1.3.6:** Connect ideas and guide the reader through transitional expressions? (Y/N)

## SLO 2: Cultural Analysis

Upon completion of the Spanish major, students will be able to analyze and contextualize a variety of cultural productions of Spanish-speaking cultures and communities.

### 2.1 Analyze

**Outcome:** Analyze the form and content of literary, artistic, and other cultural productions.

**Assessment:** Cultural analysis, SPA 315–319 regional courses

**Rubric questions:**

In a text or cultural artifact, can the student:

- **2.1.1:** Summarize or describe who, what, when, and where with supporting details? (Y/N)
- **2.1.2:** Identify themes? (Y/N)
- **2.1.3:** Locate a voice or lens with supporting details? (Y/N)
- **2.1.4:** Identify literary or artistic features? (Y/N)
- **2.1.5:** Determine how literary or artistic features contribute to the basic understanding of a theme and purpose? (Y/N)

### 2.2 Contextualize

**Outcome:** Interpret cultural productions and the perspectives they represent by applying knowledge of historical, geographical, political, and social contexts.

**Assessment:** Cultural contextualization, SPA 330–369 literature and culture seminars

**Rubric questions:**

In a text or cultural artifact, can the student:

- **2.2.1:** Identify basic facts about the region where it originates, such as historical, geographical, or cultural facts? (Y/N)
- **2.2.2:** Connect themes to a region’s settings or conditions? (Y/N)
- **2.2.3:** Deduce the perspective of a voice or lens? (Y/N)
- **2.2.4:** Compare and contrast other perspectives with that of the voice or lens? (Y/N)

### 2.3 Reflect

**Outcome:** Reflect on experiences with products in Spanish-speaking cultures to recognize cultural assumptions and suspend judgment.

**Assessment:** Cultural reflection, Assessment Day (SPA 396)

**Rubric questions:**

In a journal entry or reflective essay, does the student show:

- **2.3.1:** Awareness of personal feelings in response to the cultural product? (Y/N)
- **2.3.2:** Recognition of how their cultural biases contributed to those feelings? (Y/N)
- **2.3.3:** Capacity to imagine others experiencing similar feelings toward the student’s home culture? (Y/N)
- **2.3.4:** Recognition of the value of intercultural experience? (Y/N)

## SLO 3: Language Analysis

Upon completion of the Spanish major, students will be able to analyze and contextualize language variation across the Spanish-speaking world.

### 3.1 Analyze

**Outcome:** Analyze language samples by applying knowledge of linguistic systems and language acquisition.

**Assessment:** Language analysis, SPA 320 (Introduction to Linguistics)

**Rubric questions:**

In a language sample, can the student:

- **3.1.1:** Describe phonemes and allophones using appropriate articulation parameters or IPA symbols? (Y/N)
- **3.1.2:** Analyze inflectional or derivational morphology? (Y/N)
- **3.1.3:** Determine the function of a syntactic constituent? (Y/N)
- **3.1.4:** Differentiate between descriptive and prescriptive statements about Spanish? (Y/N) *(Criterion 3.2.1 until June 2025.)*

### 3.2 Contextualize

**Outcome:** Apply knowledge of language variation to identify differences of dialect, sociolect, and register across time, regions, cultures, and communities, including historically marginalized populations.

**Assessment:** Language contextualization, SPA 370–389 linguistics and language seminars

**Rubric questions:**

In a language sample, can the student:

- **3.2.1:** Use linguistic terminology to contrast Spanish and other languages or Spanish varieties? (Y/N) *(Criterion 3.1.4 until June 2025.)*
- **3.2.2:** Connect language production to at least one relevant speaker-internal characteristic, such as age, gender, race, region, or social class? (Y/N)
  - Example: “This speaker is using *vos* because they are from Argentina.”
- **3.2.3:** Connect language production to at least one historical, societal, or linguistic influence, such as language contact, language policy, or another typologically frequent linguistic process? (Y/N)
  - Examples: “Mexicans say *elote* because of contact with Nahuatl.” *Fisno* (cf. *fino*) is an example of hypercorrection due to stigmatization of *s* aspiration.

### 3.3 Reflect

**Outcome:** Reflect on experiences using Spanish to interact with people from Spanish-speaking communities to evaluate progress and identify areas for improvement.

**Assessment:** Language reflection, SPA OPE courses (SPA 198, 304, 389, 391, 392, 393, and 395)

**Rubric questions:**

In a conversation log or interaction report, does the student show:

- **3.3.1:** Awareness of differences and similarities between their own language variety and that of their interlocutor? (Y/N)
- **3.3.2:** Openness to adapting their language use to different contexts and audiences? (Y/N)
- **3.3.3:** Ability to self-evaluate their language capacity? (Y/N)
- **3.3.4:** Ability to set goals for improvement? (Y/N)
